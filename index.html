Your Complete Guide to Becoming a Licensed Social Worker

Everything aspiring social workers need to know — from choosing a degree to passing the ASWB exam to maintaining your license. Select your state in any section below to see requirements specific to where you'll practice.

1. The Licensure Pathway

Step 1: Earn a CSWE-Accredited Degree

Start with a degree from a CSWE-accredited (Council on Social Work Education) program. A Bachelor of Social Work (BSW) qualifies you for entry-level licenses. A Master of Social Work (MSW) is required for clinical or independent practice. All accredited MSW programs require 900+ hours of supervised field education.

Step 2: Complete Supervised Clinical Hours

After your MSW, most states require thousands of post-graduate supervised clinical hours. You'll practice under the direct oversight of a fully licensed clinical social worker while holding a provisional credential.

Step 3: Pass the ASWB Licensing Exam

The Association of Social Work Boards (ASWB) administers standardized licensing exams across the U.S. and Canada. You'll take the exam level matching your degree and target license.

Step 4: Apply for Your State License

Submit your application to your state's regulatory board. Requirements like background checks and fees vary. If you relocate, ASWB exam scores transfer between states.

Step 5: Maintain Through Continuing Education

Most states require ongoing CE hours for license renewal on a biennial or triennial cycle.

Advanced Standing: If you hold a BSW from a CSWE-accredited program, many MSW programs offer “advanced standing” tracks — completing your master's in as few as 9–12 months.

4. The ASWB Licensing Exam

The standardized exam required for social work licensure across the U.S. and Canada.

Exam Overview

The ASWB exam currently consists of 170 multiple-choice questions (150 scored, 20 unscored). You have 4 hours to complete it. The exam is administered at Pearson VUE testing centers worldwide.

Content is organized into four knowledge domains:

  • Human Development, Diversity, and Behavior in the Environment
  • Assessment, Diagnosis, and Treatment Planning
  • Psychotherapy, Clinical Interventions, and Case Management
  • Professional Relationships, Values, and Ethics

August 2026 Update: Content areas will consolidate from four into three (Values & Ethics; Assessment & Planning; Intervention & Practice). Values and ethics will carry the highest weighting. More questions will use three answer options instead of four.

Study Tips & Key Resources

  • Download the free ASWB Exam Guidebook — includes content outlines, sample questions, and a study plan.
  • Take the ASWB Official Practice Test — uses questions from actual past exams.
  • Focus on applied reasoning, not memorization — practice ethical decision-making and intervention sequencing.
  • Know the NASW Code of Ethics thoroughly — ethics questions carry significant weight.

5. Continuing Education

How Continuing Education Works

CE is required for license renewal in nearly every state. Requirements include total hours per renewal cycle, mandatory topics (almost universally ethics), and rules about acceptable formats.

Choose your state to see CE hours, ethics requirements, and renewal cycle.

Where to Find Approved CE Courses: ASWB ACE · NASW CE Programs · Accredited university social work programs

Record-Keeping: Keep certificates, provider names, dates, and hours earned. Most boards conduct random audits. Retain records 2–4 years after each renewal cycle.
